Our Approach

The Play–Practice–Play Method

Every session — conditioning or practice — follows the same rhythm U.S. Soccer uses to develop young players. We start with the game, teach inside the game, and end with the game.

Phase 1 · Play

Arrive & Play

Players get on the ball the moment they arrive — small-sided games and free play warm up the body and the mind. Coaches watch and let the game flow.

Phase 2 · Practice

Train the Theme

A focused activity and small-sided game build the day's theme. Coaches use guided questions instead of long lectures, then let players solve it.

Phase 3 · Play

Play the Game

We finish where we started — a scrimmage with minimal interference, so players apply what they practiced at real game speed.

Summer Conditioning · Optional

3-Week Conditioning Program

An optional, no-pressure summer build to get Lions moving, fit, and sharp before the season. Mondays, Wednesdays, and Fridays, 5:30–7:00 PM. Every day is ball-centered and playful — we build fitness through soccer, not away from it.

Week 1

Build the Base

Reintroduce movement, teach the warm-up routine, and rebuild the technical foundation. Moderate intensity — the goal is quality movement and good habits, not exhaustion.

Week 2

Turn It Up

Add volume and intensity. Longer aerobic games, more speed and agility reps, and quicker transitions. Players start to feel their engine growing.

Week 3

Game Speed

Everything at match tempo. Competitive conditioning games, sharp finishing, and full-effort battles that carry straight into preseason practice.

Each day below is the base template. Across the three weeks, hold the structure the same and increase the work: more rounds, shorter rest, larger spaces, and higher tempo as fitness improves.

Week-to-week progression

Week 1 — run the templates at moderate effort; teach the warm-up and technique. Week 2 — add 1–2 rounds to each block, trim rest, and enlarge spaces. Week 3 — everything at game speed with competition in every block, then taper the final Friday so players arrive at preseason fresh, not flat.

Season Practice Plan

Weekly Practice Schedule

Four practices a week — Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, and Friday, 3:15–5:00 PM. Each day owns one theme and runs the full Play–Practice–Play rhythm so players connect the skill straight to the game. Tap a day for the full session detail.

Monday

Dribbling & 1v1

Beating a defender, changes of pace, and the confidence to take players on.

Tuesday

Passing & Receiving

First touch, support angles, and connecting the team through the ball.

Thursday

Finishing

Attacking the goal, combinations, and putting chances away with both feet.

Friday

Defending & Game Day

Winning the ball back, team defending, and a full game to celebrate the week.

The 105-minute rhythm stays the same every day: Play (25) → Practice: Training (30) → Practice: Small-Sided Game (25) → Play: Scrimmage (20) → Cool-down (5). Only the theme changes.

Leveling up week to week

Keep the four day-themes and the Play–Practice–Play rhythm the same all season — that consistency helps the kids. What changes is the difficulty: shrink the space for more pressure or enlarge it for more running, add or free up a defender, and tighten the conditions (fewer touches, first-time finishes, quicker restarts). Most of all, lean into whatever the last game showed you needed. Same framework, a little harder and sharper each week.

MMon
Theme · Dribbling & 1v1

Beat the Defender

3:15 – 5:00 PM · 105 min
Goal for the day

Give every player the tools and the courage to take a defender on 1v1 and win — a move, a change of pace, and an explosion into space.

You'll need

A ball per player · ~20 cones · 2 colors of bibs · 4 pop-up goals (6 ft).

Warm-up

Open with ~5 min of dynamic prep, each player with a ball: easy jog, leg swings, open-the-gate, ankle bounces and knee hugs, plus a little juggling — then flow straight into the arrival game below.

Arrival & Free Dribbling + Dribble TagPlay · 3:15–3:40 · 25 min

Set up20×20 grid, every player with a ball inside. Pick 2 taggers to start.

Run itFree dribble with heads up. On "go," taggers try to knock other balls out of the grid while protecting nothing of their own. Ball knocked out? 5 toe-taps at the edge and re-enter. New taggers every 2–3 min. Final 8 min: 1v1-to-the-line races — pair up on opposite edges, coach rolls a ball between them, first to control and dribble across the far line wins.

Coaching points
  • Small, close touches
  • Head up to scan
  • Shield the ball with your body
  • Use both feet

Ask"What can your body do to protect the ball while you're still moving?"

Listen forput your body between the ball and the defender, keep it on your far foot, and use an arm to feel and hold them off.

Make it harderShrink the grid, or add a third tagger so there's more pressure and more scanning.

Good whenPlayers change direction to escape pressure without staring at the ball.

1v1 to GatesPractice · Training · 3:40–4:10 · 30 min

Set upSeveral channels ~10×8 yds, a gate (2 cones) on each end line. Attacker with a ball at one end, defender starting from the far gate. Run multiple channels at once so nobody waits.

Run itAttacker beats the defender and dribbles through either end gate to score. Teach one move per round — (1) change of pace, (2) scissor / step-over, (3) chop / cut-back. Demo slowly with both feet, then go live. Rotate attacker↔defender every 2 goes; switch partners each round.

Coaching points
  • Sell the fake with body and eyes
  • Explode right after the move, not during it
  • First touch into space, not at the defender
  • Attack the defender's front foot

Ask"When do you explode past the defender — before or after the move?"

Listen forafter — you burst the instant the defender leans the wrong way. The move sets the trap; the acceleration beats them.

Make it harderAdd a second gate to attack so the attacker chooses which way to beat the defender; add a small-goal finish right after the take-on.

Good whenPlayers commit to a move at pace and accelerate through, instead of slow-dribbling at the defender.

3v3 to Small Goals — take-on bonusPractice · SSG · 4:10–4:35 · 25 min

Set up25×20 grid, two pop-up goals (6 ft) on each end line, no keepers. Teams of 3; extras rest one game and rotate in.

Run itNormal 3v3. Rule: a goal scored right after a successful 1v1 take-on counts double. 4–5 minute games, winners stay on.

Coaching points
  • Be brave, take defenders on in tight space
  • Support the teammate who beats their man
  • Attack the space behind a beaten defender

Ask"Once you've beaten your defender, where's the space to attack?"

Listen forthe gap the defender just left — attack it toward goal before help arrives, and spot the teammate whose defender stepped over to cover.

Good whenPlayers choose to take defenders on inside a real game, not just in the drill.

5v5 / 7v7 ScrimmagePlay · 4:35–4:55 · 20 min

Set upTwo even teams by bib color; biggest space you have, real goals or wide gates. With a full squad, run two smaller games side by side or rotate subs every few minutes so nobody sits.

Run itLet them play with minimal stoppages. One quiet reminder at most: "look for your 1v1 when you get isolated."

Ask (only if needed)"Can you take him on right there?"

Listen foryes when you're 1v1 in space with cover behind you; no when you're the last defender back or badly outnumbered.

Good whenThe dribbling shows up on its own at game speed.

Cool-down & HuddleCool-down · 4:55–5:00 · 5 min

Run itStatic stretch ~20s each: calves, quads, hamstrings, hips. Hydrate.

Huddle"What worked when you took a player on today?" One or two players share.

Theme · Passing & Receiving

Connect the Team

3:15 – 5:00 PM · 105 min
Goal for the day

A first touch that opens up the field, and crisp passing with movement so the team can keep the ball under pressure.

You'll need

A ball per player · ~20 cones · 2 colors of bibs · a few pinnies for "targets" / neutrals.

Warm-up

Open with ~5 min of dynamic prep, each player with a ball: easy jog, leg swings, open-the-gate, ankle bounces and knee hugs, plus a little juggling — then flow straight into the arrival game below.

Rondo Warm-Up — 4v1 / 5v2 Keep-AwayPlay · 3:15–3:40 · 25 min

Set upSmall squares ~8×8 yds; 4–5 players around the outside, 1–2 defenders inside.

Run itOutside players keep the ball away; whoever loses it or misplays a pass swaps into the middle. Start two-touch. Fast, fun, full of reps before you've said a word.

Coaching points
  • Open your body to see the whole square
  • Pass to your teammate's back (far) foot
  • First touch out of your feet
  • Move a step after every pass

Ask"Which angle lets you receive and still see the whole field?"

Listen foran open, side-on angle off the passing line, so you see the ball and the field at once — never flat or hidden behind a defender.

Make it harderOne-touch for confident groups, or shrink the square.

Good whenThe ball zips, angles keep opening, and the middle defenders are chasing.

Passing Patterns → 3v1 / 4v2 GridsPractice · Training · 3:40–4:10 · 30 min

Set up4–5 cones in a Y or diamond for patterns; then small ~10×10 grids for live 3v1 / 4v2.

Run it · patternsPut a player at each cone (extras wait at the start cone). The first player passes to the next cone in the shape, then follows their pass and joins that line. The receiver takes a touch across the body and plays on to the next cone, so the ball travels around the Y or diamond nonstop. Both feet, about 5 minutes, then switch directions.

Run it · liveNow split into small ~10×10 grids for keep-away. In 3v1, three attackers keep the ball from one defender (4v2 = four vs two). Score a point each time you connect a set number of passes (start at 5). Lose it, or knock it out of the grid, and the player who lost it goes in the middle — in 4v2, rotate the defenders. About 6–8 minutes.

Coaching points
  • Pass to the back foot
  • Receive with your back foot, across your body
  • Check to the ball, then show for it
  • Firm, accurate weight of pass
  • Both feet

Ask"Which way should your first touch go before the ball even arrives?"

Listen foraway from pressure and toward your next pass — decide before it arrives and take your touch into space, not onto your toes.

Make it harderLimit to two touches, then one; add a second defender.

Good whenThe first touch already sets up the next pass and players keep adjusting their support angles.

4v4 Possession + End-Zone TargetsPractice · SSG · 4:10–4:35 · 25 min

Set up30×24 grid with a 3-yd end zone at each end; one target player per team waiting in the far end zone.

Run itConnect 5 passes, then play into your target in the end zone for a point; the target returns it and play continues. Rotate the target every couple of minutes.

Coaching points
  • Be patient, keep the ball
  • Switch the point of attack to the open side
  • Move off the ball to create angles
  • Support at an angle, not flat

Ask"How did you get open for your teammate just now?"

Listen foryou moved — a check away and back, or a step into the gap between two defenders. You make the passing lane with your feet.

Make it harderAdd a neutral player for the team in possession, or drop the pass count but add a defender.

Good whenThe team keeps the ball and finds the target through movement, not by forcing it.

ScrimmagePlay · 4:35–4:55 · 20 min

Set upTwo even teams, goals, biggest space available. With a full squad, run two games side by side or keep subs rotating so everyone plays.

Run itEncourage playing out and connecting, but let them play freely.

Ask (only if needed)"Can we string three passes before we go forward?"

Listen foryes when we stay patient and give the ball-carrier two support angles; the third pass is usually the one that opens the forward ball.

Good whenPlayers look to connect and support instead of booting it clear.

Cool-down & HuddleCool-down · 4:55–5:00 · 5 min

Run itStatic stretch ~20s each: calves, quads, hamstrings, hips. Hydrate.

Huddle"How did you get open for a teammate today?"

Theme · Finishing

Attack the Goal

3:15 – 5:00 PM · 105 min
Goal for the day

Composed, both-footed finishing and a clear head in the box — knowing when to shoot and when to slide the extra pass.

You'll need

A ball per player · 2 full goals · goalkeepers (or rotate players in) · ~16 cones · bibs.

Warm-up

Open with ~5 min of dynamic prep, each player with a ball: easy jog, leg swings, open-the-gate, ankle bounces and knee hugs, plus a little juggling — then flow straight into the arrival game below.

Finishing Lines + Free Play on GoalsPlay · 3:15–3:40 · 25 min

Set upTwo shooting lines, left and right, ~16–18 yds out; open nets or keepers; a ball each. Keep both lines going so nobody waits.

Run itDribble in and finish, placement first, then retrieve a ball and switch lines. Roll into small games to goal to wake up the striker's instinct.

Coaching points
  • Plant foot beside the ball
  • Side-foot or laces for placement
  • Head steady, eyes on the ball
  • Aim for the far corner / back post

Ask"Placement or power from here — which scores more often?"

Listen forplacement — a firm, accurate side-foot to the corner beats blasting it at the keeper. Save power for when you have a big, open angle.

Make it harderOne-touch finishes off a served ball.

Good whenShots are on frame and into the corners, off both feet.

2v1 & 3v2 to GoalPractice · Training · 3:40–4:10 · 30 min

Set upOne full goal with a keeper; attackers start ~25 yds out, defender(s) start goal-side; feeder balls at a cone.

Run itAttackers combine — give-and-go or overlap — to break the defense and finish. Reset and rotate roles quickly so reps stay high.

Coaching points
  • Draw the defender in, then release the pass
  • Time your run to stay onside
  • Finish first-time when the keeper is moving
  • Follow your shot for rebounds

Ask"When is it time to shoot, and when do you play the extra pass?"

Listen forshoot when you have a clear look and the keeper's beaten; pass when a teammate has the better angle. A square ball into an open net beats a hopeful shot.

Make it harderAdd a recovering defender who chases from behind to force quicker decisions.

Good whenThey use the extra attacker and finish calmly instead of forcing a low-odds shot.

4v4 + Keepers to Full Goals — first-time bonusPractice · SSG · 4:10–4:35 · 25 min

Set up30×25 grid with two full goals and keepers.

Run itNormal game; first-time finishes count double. Fast transitions, lots of shots.

Coaching points
  • Get numbers forward quickly
  • Serve the ball in early
  • Crash the goal for rebounds
  • Shoot first-time when you can

Ask"Who's attacking the back post for the rebound?"

Listen forthe far-side attacker — always run to the back post as the shot is struck; that's where most rebounds and cut-backs land.

Good whenPlayers shoot early and gamble on rebounds instead of over-passing.

Scrimmage on GoalsPlay · 4:35–4:55 · 20 min

Set upTwo even teams, full goals, biggest space available. With a full squad, run two games side by side or rotate subs often so no one sits.

Run itFull game — reward getting numbers forward to attack. Mostly observe.

Good whenThe team commits runners forward and creates real chances.

Huddle"Where on the goal are you aiming — and why?"

Win It Back

3:15 – 5:00 PM · 105 min
Goal for the day

Defend well as an individual and as a group — pressure, patience, and cover — then let it rip in a full game to celebrate the week.

You'll need

A ball per player · ~20 cones · 2 colors of bibs · pop-up goals + 2 full goals for game day.

Warm-up

Open with ~5 min of dynamic prep, each player with a ball: easy jog, leg swings, open-the-gate, ankle bounces and knee hugs, plus a little juggling — then flow straight into the arrival game below.

Free Play + 1v1 Defending Warm-UpPlay · 3:15–3:40 · 25 min

Set upGrid for free dribbling, then pair players into short channels.

Run itTouches first, then 1v1 in channels: the attacker tries to reach the end line, the defender jockeys and delays without diving in.

Coaching points
  • Approach on a curve
  • Show the attacker one way
  • Stay on your toes, side-on
  • Be patient, don't dive in

Ask"Which way are you steering the attacker, and why?"

Listen fortoward the sideline or onto their weaker foot — away from goal and the middle, so they have fewer options and help can arrive.

Good whenDefenders slow the attacker and force the mistake instead of lunging in.

1v1 & 2v2 DefendingPractice · Training · 3:40–4:10 · 30 min

Set upChannels for 1v1; then 2v2 grids with pop-up goals to defend.

Run itStart with 1v1 pressure and angle work, then build to 2v2 — first defender pressures the ball, second covers behind. Talk: "you take ball, I've got cover."

Coaching points
  • First defender pressures the ball
  • Second defender covers behind and inside
  • Keep the right distance (about a stride away)
  • Delay to buy time for help
  • Win it, then keep it

Ask"How close do you get before you slow down — and who's covering behind you?"

Listen forto about a stride away, then break down on your toes; the second defender sits a few yards behind and inside, ready to cover if the first is beaten.

Make it harderGo 3v3 with a target player to spring a counter the moment they win the ball.

Good whenPairs defend together and immediately turn a steal into an attack.

4v4 / 5v5 Win-It-Back Game (6 seconds)Practice · SSG · 4:10–4:35 · 25 min

Set up30×25 grid with goals.

Run itNormal game with one twist: the team that just lost the ball earns a bonus point if they win it back within 6 seconds. Count it out loud.

Coaching points
  • Nearest player presses immediately
  • Cut the passing lanes
  • Collapse toward the ball together
  • Win it back within 6 seconds

Ask"The second we lose it — who presses first?"

Listen forthe closest player to the ball presses instantly while everyone else squeezes in to cut the passing lanes — that's how we win it back in six seconds.

Good whenThe whole team reacts instantly on turnovers instead of jogging back.

Coach's How-To Guide

Every Drill, Explained Simply

You don't need a coaching badge to run a great session. This guide breaks down every warm-up, drill, and term in the plan above — what it is, how to set it up in seconds, and what "good" looks like — so you can walk onto the field and coach any day with confidence.

Decode the Lingo

Soccer Words, in Plain English

These are the terms sprinkled through the plan. Read these once and the rest of the guide clicks into place.

Small-sided game (SSG)

Any mini game with fewer players than a real match — 3v3, 4v4, etc. Fewer players means every kid touches the ball far more often. The heart of the whole method.

Grid

A square or rectangle marked with 4 cones — the playing area for a drill. Bigger grid = more running; smaller grid = more touches and pressure. You'll size it up or down all the time.

Gate

Two cones set about 1–2 steps apart, making a mini doorway. Players "score" by dribbling or passing through it. Scatter several around a grid.

Surfaces of the foot

The parts of the foot you touch the ball with: inside (passing & control), laces / top (dribbling fast & shooting), outside (quick touches while running), sole / bottom (stopping & rolling).

Take-on

Beating a defender by dribbling past them one-on-one. When we "reward a take-on," we're encouraging kids to be brave and try it.

Transition

The moment the ball changes teams — you just lost it (defend now!) or just won it (attack now!). Fast transitions win games.

Possession

Keeping the ball as a team by passing and moving. "Possession games" score points for stringing passes together, not for goals.

Give-and-go (wall pass)

Pass to a teammate and immediately sprint past your defender to get it right back — like passing off a wall. A simple 2-player combo.

Overlap

A teammate runs from behind and around you on the outside to create an extra attacker and stretch the defense.

Both feet

Practicing with the left foot as much as the right. Young players lean hard on their strong foot — we deliberately make them use both.

SAQ

Speed, Agility & Quickness — the umbrella term for short, sharp footwork and sprint drills that make players faster and more nimble.

Guided questions

Instead of telling kids the answer, you ask a short question and let them discover it by trying. "Which foot keeps the ball closest when you run fast?" They experiment, they learn, it sticks.

Warm-Up & Activation

Waking the Body Up

Two to three minutes of easy movement before anything intense. "Activation" just means gently switching on the muscles around a joint so kids don't tweak an ankle or pull a hip. No equipment needed.

Warm-Up

Jog & Skips

WhatEasy lap to raise the heart rate, then exaggerated skipping to loosen hips and legs.

Run itOne slow lap, then skip 15 yards and back, twice.

Good whenKids are moving smoothly and breathing a little harder — not sprinting yet.

Warm-Up

Walking Lunges

WhatBig forward steps, dropping the back knee toward the ground. Warms legs and hips.

Run itLunge-walk 10 steps out, jog back. Keep the front knee over the ankle, not past the toes.

Good whenSteps are controlled and balanced, chest up.

Hip Activation

Leg Swings

WhatSwinging one leg to loosen the hip — the main "hip activation" move.

Run itHold a fence or partner. Swing one leg forward & back ×10, then side to side ×10. Switch legs.

Good whenThe swing is relaxed and getting a bit bigger each rep.

Hip Activation

Open / Close the Gate

WhatWalking while rotating the knee out (open) and in (close). Loosens the hips for kicking and cutting.

Run itWalk forward; lift a knee and rotate it out to the side (open the gate), next step rotate the other knee in across the body (close the gate). 5 each way.

Good whenKnees lift to hip height and rotate smoothly.

Hip Activation

Walking Knee Hugs

WhatPulling each knee up to the chest as you walk. Wakes up hips and glutes.

Run itEach step, hug one knee to the chest and squeeze for a beat, then step and switch. 10 total.

Good whenKids stay tall and balanced instead of hunching over.

Ankle Activation

Ankle Bounces & Walks

WhatWaking up the ankles so they can push off and cut safely.

Run itWalk 10 yards on tip-toes, walk back on heels. Finish with 15–20 small quick pogo hops on the balls of the feet.

Good whenHops are light, quick, and quiet — barely touching down.

Warm-Up

Juggling

WhatKeeping the ball off the ground with feet and thighs. Builds touch, balance, and confidence — and it's fun.

Run itEveryone with a ball, count your best streak. Younger kids: allow one bounce between touches.

Good whenTouches are soft and under the ball, not swinging at it.

Cool-Down

Static Stretch

WhatSlow, held stretches at the end of a session to help recovery. (Save the deep stretching for after — the warm-up should be moving, not standing still.)

Run itHold each ~20 seconds: calves, quads, hamstrings, hips. Sip water. Quick team huddle.

Good whenEveryone's calm, hydrated, and cooled down before heading home.

Speed, Agility & Fitness

Getting Faster & Fitter

Short, sharp drills for quick feet and sprinting, plus the ball-based fitness work from the conditioning plan. Rule of thumb for speed work: full effort, full rest — quality beats quantity.

Agility

Quick-Feet Ladder

WhatA flat rope ladder on the ground; players step feet in and out fast. No ladder? Lay a line of cones 1.5 ft apart.

Run itPatterns like one foot per box, then two feet per box, then a side shuffle through. Fast feet, light on the toes.

Good whenFeet are quick and quiet and they're not looking down the whole time.

Agility

A-Skips & High Knees

WhatSprint-form drills. A-skip = a skip driving the knee up to hip height. High knees = quick running in place/forward with knees pumping up.

Run it15 yards of each. Drive knees up, pump the arms, land on the balls of the feet.

Good whenTall posture, knees up, arms driving — this is teaching good running form.

Agility

Cone Shuffles

WhatSide-to-side shuffling between two cones in an athletic stance.

Run itCones ~5 yards apart. Shuffle side to side, stay low, don't cross the feet. 3–4 trips.

Good whenHips stay low and feet stay quick underneath them.

Agility

5-10-5 (Change of Direction)

WhatA classic agility sprint: 3 cones in a line, 5 yards between each.

Run itStart at the middle cone. Sprint 5 yds to one side & touch the line, sprint 10 yds to the far cone & touch, sprint 5 yds back through the middle.

Good whenThey plant hard, stay low in the turns, and explode out — not rounding the corners.

Speed

Reaction Sprints & Build-Ups

WhatSprints that start on your signal (reaction) and "accelerations" that build gradually to ~70% to prep the legs.

Run itLine them up; point or call a direction and they sprint 10–15 yds. For build-ups, jog→stride→ease off, ×3.

Good whenThey react instantly and run relaxed, not tense.

Conditioning

Shuttle Runs

WhatSprint to a line and back, repeatedly. Simple fitness plus change-of-direction.

Run itCones at 5, 10, 15 yards. Sprint to each and back. Rest, repeat 4–6 times.

Good whenEffort holds up on the later reps — that's the fitness building.

Conditioning

Relay Races & Obstacle Course

WhatTeam races — dribbling relays, shuttle relays, or weaving a ball through cones/over small hurdles. Competitive fitness that feels like a game.

Run itSplit into even teams, one ball per team, first team to finish wins. Keep lines short so kids rest, then go hard.

Good whenEnergy is high, effort is real, and control doesn't fall apart in the rush.

Conditioning

Aerobic Dribble Intervals

WhatBuilding endurance with the ball instead of just running laps.

Run itDribble at a strong pace for 2 minutes (change direction on the whistle, use both feet), then 1 minute easy recovery. Repeat 6–8 times.

Good whenThe ball stays close even as they tire and legs get heavy.

Speed + Ball

1v1 Speed Duels to Goal

WhatTwo players race for a ball, then attack a goal 1v1. Explosive speed inside a real soccer moment.

Run itTwo lines behind cones ~15 yds from goal. Coach rolls a ball out; both sprint — whoever gets it attacks, the other defends. Keeper in goal. Rest between reps.

Good whenFirst touch pushes the ball toward goal and they accelerate onto it.

Ball & Technical Work

Skills on the Ball

The touches, moves, and technique drills. The golden rule: everyone has a ball, nobody stands in long lines. More touches = faster learning.

Technique

Ball Mastery

WhatRepetitive touches to get comfortable — inside-outside (push the ball with the inside of the foot, then the outside), sole rolls (roll it side to side with the bottom of the foot), toe taps on top.

Run itEveryone with a ball in a grid, 30–45 seconds per move, both feet.

Good whenTouches are relaxed and heads start to come up off the ball.

Technique

Footwork Ladder + Ball

WhatQuick feet through the ladder, then dribble out — links foot speed to controlling the ball.

Run itDo a ladder pattern, then dribble a ball waiting at the end through a few cones.

Good whenThe quick feet carry straight into a controlled dribble.

Game

Dribble Tag

WhatA fun warm-up game — everyone dribbles in a grid while 1–2 "taggers" try to kick others' balls out, all while protecting their own.

Run itEveryone with a ball inside a grid. If your ball gets knocked out, do 3 toe-taps and rejoin. Rotate taggers.

Good whenKids keep their heads up and shield the ball with their body.

1v1 Moves

Scissor & Step-Over

WhatFake moves to beat a defender. A scissor/step-over is swinging a foot around the front of the ball (without touching it) to sell a fake, then pushing the ball away with the other foot.

Run itPractice slowly with no defender first, both feet, then add a passive defender to beat.

Good whenThe fake is followed by a quick burst — the move only works with the acceleration after it.

1v1 Moves

Change of Pace

WhatThe simplest, most effective move: slow down to lull the defender, then explode past them.

Run itDribble at a defender, ease off, then push the ball into space and sprint.

Good whenThe speed change is obvious — slow, then GO.

Technique

Receiving "Across the Body" / Open Up

WhatTaking your first touch with the far foot into open space so you can see the field — instead of trapping the ball dead in front of you under pressure.

Run itAs the pass arrives, touch it with the foot farthest from the ball, pushing it out to your side and turning to face forward.

Good whenAfter one touch they're facing up the field, not with their back to it.

Technique

Passing Patterns

WhatPassing in a set sequence — "pass and follow your pass" — to groove good technique and movement.

Run itSet 4–5 cones in a shape; players pass along the pattern, then jog to the cone they passed to. Both feet.

Good whenPasses are firm and accurate and players move immediately after passing.

Finishing

Finishing Lines

WhatOrganized shooting reps at goal, both feet — accuracy first, then power.

Run itA short line of players; each dribbles in and shoots, then retrieves a ball. Keep two lines going (left & right) so nobody waits long.

Good whenThey're placing shots to the corners and hitting with the laces, not toe-poking.

Small-Sided Games

The Games (and How to Set Them Up)

This is where it all comes together. Set the field, split even teams, give the game a simple rule, then mostly step back and let them play. A few coaching questions here and there beat constant whistle-blowing.

Possession

Rondo / Keep-Away (4v1, 5v2)

WhatThe classic "monkey in the middle." Outside players keep the ball; the defender(s) in the middle try to win it.

Set upSmall circle or square of players, 1–2 defenders inside.

Run itKeep possession with quick passes. Whoever loses the ball (or misplaces a pass) swaps into the middle.

Good whenPassers open up angles and the ball zips around.

1v1

1v1 to a Line / Gate

WhatOne attacker tries to beat one defender by dribbling across a line or through a gate.

Set upA small channel; attacker at one end with a ball, defender facing them.

Run itAttacker scores by dribbling over the far line / through a gate. Swap roles each go.

Good whenAttackers try their moves & changes of pace; defenders stay patient, don't dive in.

Mini Game

3v3 to Small Goals

WhatA tiny match, no goalkeepers, two pop-up goals (6 ft) per end.

Set upGrid about 25×20 yards, two pop-up goals (6 ft) on each end line.

Run itPlay a normal game. Add a rule to fit the day — e.g., "a goal after a take-on counts double."

Good whenEveryone's involved and the rule is nudging the behavior you want.

Possession

4v4 Possession + End-Zone Targets

WhatKeep the ball, then find a "target" teammate in an end zone to score a point.

Set upGrid with an end zone at each end; one target player per team stands in the opposite end zone.

Run itConnect a set number of passes, then pass into your target for a point. Rotate the target.

Good whenPlayers move to support and stay patient instead of forcing it.

Attacking

2v1 & 3v2 to Goal

WhatAttackers outnumber defenders and attack a real goal — teaches the shoot-or-pass decision with an advantage.

Set upOne goal with a keeper; attackers start ~25 yds out, defender(s) start goal-side.

Run itAttackers go for goal using give-and-goes or overlaps. Reset and rotate quickly.

Good whenThey use the extra player — a pass to the open teammate — rather than forcing a shot.

Transition

Transition Game to 4 Goals

WhatEach team defends two goals and attacks two — spreads the field and forces quick switching and transitions.

Set upA wider grid with two pop-up goals (6 ft) on each end line (4 total).

Run itScore in either of the two goals you're attacking. When possession flips, everyone reacts fast.

Good whenPlayers switch play to the open goal and sprint the moment the ball turns over.

Defending

Win-It-Back Game (6 seconds)

WhatA normal small game with one twist that teaches team defending.

Set upAny 4v4 / 5v5 setup with goals.

Run itThe team that just lost the ball earns a bonus point if they win it back within 6 seconds. Count it out loud.

Good whenPlayers pressure together immediately instead of jogging back.

Competition

Tournament — Winners Stay

WhatShort, back-to-back mini games with stakes. Great for competitive conditioning.

Set upTwo or three tiny fields, teams of 3.

Run it4-minute games; the winning team stays on, the next team rotates in. Everyone plays every round.

Good whenGames are quick, teams are even, and there's real energy.

Free Play

Scrimmage

WhatA regular game — the final "Play" phase where kids apply what they practiced.

Set upEven teams, goals, biggest space you have.

Run itLet them play. Coach mostly watches; drop in one quick question, not a running commentary.

Good whenYou see the day's theme show up on its own. That's the win.

Field Setup & Coaching Basics

The 5-Minute Setup

Everything you need to run a session smoothly, keep it fair, and keep it safe.

What to pack

A simple bag covers every drill in the plan.

~20 cones2 sets of colored bibs/pinniesA ball per playerPop-up goals (or use cones)Water for breaksThis plan on your phone

Cones do everything

Four cones = a grid (the play area). Two cones close together = a gate or a pop-up goal. A line of cones = a ladder or a dribbling course. You rarely need anything fancier. Make the space bigger for more running and passing, smaller for more touches and pressure.

Our goals

We have two full-size goals, fixed at the ends, plus four 6-ft pop-up goals you can move anywhere. Use the full-size goals for finishing work and the scrimmage, and the pop-up goals for the small-goal games (3v3, 2v2, 4-goal). Cone gates (two cones) can cover any extra targets you need.

Making even teams fast

Hand out bibs as kids arrive to split colors evenly, and mix ability and age across teams. If a game is lopsided, don't stop it — just swap one player or add a rule that helps the team that's behind. Keep groups small (3v3, 4v4) so nobody hides and everyone touches the ball.

The one habit that makes you look like a pro

Ask, don't tell. When you see something, resist explaining for 30 seconds and instead ask a short question — "What could you try there?" — then let them figure it out in the next rep. Kids remember what they discover far better than what they're told.

For Parents & Players

What to Know

A few notes to keep our Lions safe, healthy, and enjoying the game all summer and season long.

Come ready

Bring these to every session so players get the most out of their time on the field.

Size 5 ballFull water bottleShin guardsCleats + flatsSunscreenWeather-appropriate layers

Health & wellbeing first

Summer conditioning is optional and no-pressure — come when you can. We take water breaks every 15–20 minutes, watch the heat, and keep intensity age-appropriate for grades 4–8. Rest and recovery between sessions matter as much as the work itself. If a player is hurt or unwell, we sit them out — no exceptions.

Our coaching promise

We coach the U.S. Soccer grassroots way: maximum touches on the ball, the game as the best teacher, guided questions over long lectures, and every player getting real playing time. We're developing soccer players and young people of character — the scoreboard comes second to growth, effort, and joy in the game.
